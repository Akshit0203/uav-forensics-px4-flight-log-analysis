# Stepwise Drone Assembly (F450 Quadcopter)

> This module covers the physical assembly of an F450 quadcopter — from frame to flight controller — and explains how navigation hardware supports route tracking, stabilization, and event logging. Understanding assembly is essential for forensic disassembly and knowing where relevant data is stored.

## Module Topics

1. Understanding the Flight Controller (FC)
2. Sensors: IMU, Camera, GPS
3. Programming FC with Mission Planner
4. Onboard data storage
5. Memory crucial for flight data recording

# Step 1: Frame Assembly & Soldering to PDB

The F450 lower plate acts as an **integrated Power Distribution Board (PDB)** with internal traces already laid out on the PCB.

### Screws Included in F450 Kit

| Screw | Size | Thread | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short screws | 6 mm | M2.5 | Attaching arms to frame plates |
| Long screws | 8 mm | M3 | Mounting motors to arms |

> Screws come with **pre-applied thread locker**.

### Soldering Tasks

1. Solder battery leads (or a female connector) to the PDB.
2. Solder the **positive (red)** and **negative (black)** power leads of each ESC to the corresponding solder pads on the PDB.

---

# Step 2: Attach 4 ESCs to the PDB Connectors

## Connector Types on Drone Components

Selecting the right connector depends on your drone's **current draw**.

### Battery-to-ESC Connectors

| Connector | Max Current | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **XT60** | Up to 60A | **Industry standard** for medium-draw setups. Yellow connector. |
| **XT90** | Up to 90A | For high current draw. Longer than XT60. |
| **Deans T-Plug** | Up to 60A | Older legacy style, red connector. Available as fireproof pairs. |

### Motor-to-ESC Connectors

| Connector | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Bullet / Banana connectors** (3.5mm or 4.0mm) | Soldered directly to the 3 ESC wires. Gold-colored. |
| **MT30 / MT60 multi-pin plugs** | Integrated connectors covered with heat-shrink tubing to prevent shorts. |

> **Rule of thumb:** Place **female connectors on the ESC side** and **male connectors on the motor side** to prevent accidental battery short circuits.

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705233030.png)

> **Tip:** Always keep extra connectors on hand — you will need to replace arms, motors, or ESCs.

# Step 3: Arm Mounting, Orientation & Wiring

### Attaching Arms

- Bolt the four arms to the lower plate using the short M2.5 screws and an Allen key.
- **Color convention:** Two **white/black arms** for the front, two **red arms** for the rear (or vice versa — the key is visual distinction during flight).
- White arms as front provide better daytime visibility against the sky.
- For **night flying**, always mount LEDs on the quadcopter.

### Landing Legs

- Screw on the landing legs, ensuring the ESC power connectors run **through the arch** formed by the leg mounting points on the PDB (so wires don't hang loose).

### Mounting ESCs on Arms

- **Zip-tie** or tape the 30A ESCs along the top of each arm.
- Use an **extra zip tie** at each end of the arm to keep wiring neat and within the frame enclosure.

> ⚠️ **Loose wires can get entangled in propellers and cause a crash.** Always secure wiring with zip ties.

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705233951.png)

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705234139.png)

### Label the ESC Signal Connectors

Before adding the top plate, **number the ESC control cable connectors** (1, 2, 3, 4) using a silver/golden/white permanent marker.

> Labeling is essential for troubleshooting and correct flight controller wiring.

# Step 4: Motor Numbering, Rotation & Mounting

## Motor Numbering Layout (Mission Planner Standard)

![421](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705234348.png)

With the **front of the drone** (nose) facing forward:

| Position | Motor # | Rotation |
|---|---|---|
| Front Right | **Motor 1** | CCW ↺ |
| Rear Left | **Motor 2** | CCW ↺ |
| Front Left | **Motor 3** | CW ↻ |
| Rear Right | **Motor 4** | CW ↻ |

- Motors **1 & 2** (diagonal pair) = **Counter-clockwise (CCW)**
- Motors **3 & 4** (diagonal pair) = **Clockwise (CW)**
- The A, B, C, D labels in the diagram are used later during motor testing.

> ⚠️ **This numbering follows Mission Planner's standard exactly.** You must follow it strictly.

## Mounting Motors

![452](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705234558.png)

- Each motor is labeled with **CW or CCW** text and an arrow showing its intended rotation.
- Mount motors on arms according to the numbering/rotation layout above.

### Why Correct Rotation Direction Matters

- A BLDC motor has 3 wires — **swapping any two wires reverses its spin direction**. So a CW motor *can* spin CCW, but it shouldn't.
- If a motor spins in the **intended direction**, the rotational force naturally **tightens the propeller nut** (self-tightening, like car wheel threads).
- If spun in the **wrong direction**, the nut **unscrews and the propeller flies off**.

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260705234901.png)

### Motor-to-ESC Connection

- Open the second screw packet (longer 8mm M3 screws).
- Pair each arm with the motor having the **appropriate rotation** for that position.
- **Solder or plug** the three motor wires into the three ESC wires.
  - Hobbyists often **cut long motor wires to size and solder directly** for a cleaner build.
- Flatten wires down slightly to avoid pinching when screwing the motor into place.
- **4 screws per motor × 4 motors = 16 motor screws total**.

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706001742.png)

> Don't worry about exact rotation direction at this stage — it can be corrected later by swapping two of the three wires.

### Screw Count Summary

| Screws | Count | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Short M2.5 (6mm) | 12 | Arms to plates |
| Long M3 (8mm) | 16 | Motors to arms |

> Secure firmly but **do not overtighten** until everything is aligned. Then perform final tightening.

# Step 5: Top Plate Installation

![552](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706001523.png)

Place the upper frame plate over the assembly and tighten all remaining screws.

> **Why the top plate?** It **protects the flight controller** between the two plates. Even in a crash, the FC is shielded. Without it, a crash can destroy the flight controller (learned the hard way — drone hit a utility pole and the FC was destroyed).

# Step 6: Flight Controller & Receiver Installation

## Assembly Summary Diagram

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706002002.png)

**Signal flow recap:**
- Battery → 4 ESCs → 4 BLDC motors (3 wires + bullet connectors) → Propellers
- ESC signal wires (labeled 1-4) → Flight Controller
- Flight Controller → GPS, servo outputs, receiver inputs

## Install Flight Controller

![320](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706002548.png)

- **Current standard:** Pixhawk (~₹6,000–7,000 on Robu.in). APM 2.4 is rarely used now.
- Mount the FC at the **exact geometric center** of the lower plate.
- Use an **anti-vibration mount** secured with **double-sided foam tape** and zip ties.
- Ensure the **forward arrow** on both the anti-vibration mount and the FC point toward the **front of the drone** (between the two front arms).

> ⚠️ **If the IMU/magnetometer is offset by even 1 cm, the quadcopter will not fly properly.** You can compensate with transmitter trim switches, but it's far better to center the board precisely from the start.

### Wire ESCs to Flight Controller

Plug the PWM signal wires (labeled 1–4) into the **main output ports M1, M2, M3, M4** on the flight controller. Other ports are for auxiliary outputs.

## Install Radio Receiver

- Mount the **FlySky FS-iA6B** receiver (~₹1,600–1,800) behind or next to the flight controller.
- **APM boards:** Connect all 4 channels individually (CH1=Roll, CH2=Pitch, CH3=Throttle, CH4=Yaw).
- **Pixhawk:** Only a **single cable** (PPM or S.Bus) runs from the receiver to the RC input port. Usually included with the Pixhawk.

### Flight Controller Options by Budget

| Type | Casing | Price Range |
|---|---|---|
| Standard (Pixhawk) | Plastic | ~₹6,000–7,000 |
| High-end | Rugged aluminum | ~₹15,000–16,000 |

# Final Notes

> **Do NOT attach the battery and propellers until calibration is complete.** These are always installed **last**.

# Exam / Interview Points

### Assembly Order
1. Solder battery leads + ESC power leads to PDB
2. Attach ESCs to PDB connectors
3. Mount arms to lower plate (color-coded front/rear)
4. Attach landing legs
5. Zip-tie ESCs to arms, label signal connectors (1-4)
6. Mount motors (correct CW/CCW per layout)
7. Connect motor wires to ESC wires
8. Install top plate
9. Mount flight controller (center, arrow forward)
10. Wire ESC signals to FC output ports (M1-M4)
11. Mount receiver, connect to FC
12. **Last:** Battery + Propellers

### Key Facts
- **F450** = 450mm diagonal frame
- **M3 screws** (8mm) for motors | **M2.5 screws** (6mm) for arms
- **XT60** = standard battery connector (up to 60A)
- Female connector on ESC, male on motor (prevents shorts)
- Motor numbering follows **Mission Planner standard** (not sequential around frame)
- Motors 1 & 2 = CCW | Motors 3 & 4 = CW
- Swapping any **2 of 3 motor wires** reverses spin direction
- Correct rotation = propeller nut **self-tightens** | Wrong = nut unscrews, prop flies off
- FC must be at **exact geometric center**, arrow pointing forward
- **Pixhawk** = current standard FC (~₹6-7K) | APM = legacy, unsupported
- Pixhawk uses **single PPM/S.Bus cable** to receiver (APM needed 4 channels)
- Top plate protects FC in crashes
