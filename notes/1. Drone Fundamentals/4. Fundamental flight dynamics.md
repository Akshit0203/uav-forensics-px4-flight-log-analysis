# How Different Aircraft Fly

![529](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260704214344.png)

### Aeroplane vs Helicopter vs Quadcopter – Lift Generation

| Aircraft | How Lift is Generated | Rotor Plane |
|---|---|---|
| **Aeroplane** | Lift is generated by the **wing's airfoil shape**. The engine only provides forward speed (thrust). | Rotors work in the **vertical plane** (propellers pull/push forward). |
| **Helicopter** | The **wings themselves rotate** (rotor blades), creating a lift cone. A **tail rotor** mitigates the rotating torque of the main rotor. | Rotors work in the **horizontal plane**. |
| **Quadcopter** | Four motors with propellers provide thrust directly. Counter-rotating pairs cancel torque. | Rotors work in the **horizontal plane**. |

> **Key Difference:** In an aeroplane, rotors work in the **vertical plane**; in copters (helicopter / quadcopter), rotors work in the **horizontal plane**.

# How a Quadcopter Works

## Lift

1. Four duct fans (lifting fans) provide the thrust.
2. Each fan is connected through an **Electronic Speed Controller (ESC)** to a single battery source through a **power distribution board**.
3. Rotors 1 and 3 spin in **one direction**, while rotors 2 and 4 spin in the **opposite direction** — yielding opposing torques for control.

## Hover / Altitude Adjustment

A quadrotor **hovers** or adjusts its altitude by applying **equal thrust to all four rotors**.

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260704214432.png)

> In the above figure, the arrows denote the **direction of rotation** of the propellers.

---

## Yaw (Rotation Around Vertical Axis)

A quadrotor adjusts its **yaw** by applying **more thrust to rotors rotating in one direction** (e.g., increase thrust on CW pair, decrease on CCW pair).

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260704214457.png)

> In the above figure, the **thickness of the arrows** indicates more thrust on those rotors.

---

## Pitch & Roll (Tilting Forward/Backward & Left/Right)

A quadrotor adjusts its **pitch or roll** by applying **more thrust to one rotor** (or two adjacent rotors) and **less thrust to the diametrically opposite rotor**.

# Exam / Interview Points

- **Aileron** → Roll | **Elevator** → Pitch | **Rudder** → Yaw
- Aeroplane lift = wing shape (airfoil); engine provides forward thrust only
- Helicopter lift = rotating blades (lift cone); tail rotor counters torque
- Quadcopter lift = 4 motors; counter-rotating pairs cancel torque
- **ESC** = Electronic Speed Controller (one per motor)
- Hover = equal thrust on all 4 rotors
- Yaw = differential thrust on CW vs CCW rotor pairs
- Pitch/Roll = differential thrust on opposite rotors
- Aeroplane rotors → **vertical plane**; Copter rotors → **horizontal plane**
