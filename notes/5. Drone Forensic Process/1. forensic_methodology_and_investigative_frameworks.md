
# Forensic Methodology & Investigative Frameworks

---

## Drone Forensic Process — Six Phases

| Phase | Activity | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **1** | Information Gathering | Incident details, actors, drone model, GCS type |
| **2** | Preparation | Tool readiness, imaging, chain of custody |
| **3** | Identification | Component inventory, physical & digital evidence |
| **4** | Forensic Analysis | Bit-by-bit imaging, data extraction & examination |
| **5** | Reporting | Artifact review, event documentation, final report |
| **6** | Presentation | Court testimony, legal-clause correlation |

---

## Phase 1 — Information Gathering

The process begins with gathering information about:
- The **incident** itself
- **Potential actors** involved
- **Type of drone** that caused the incident
- **Ground Control Station (GCS)** used by the operator
- Any other leads that help in deciding a sound investigative approach

---

## Phase 2 — Preparation

- Identify the **specific drone model** to determine which parsers will be required and whether onboard data is encrypted.
- Have all appropriate **imaging tools ready beforehand**.
- Prepare resources for **forensically seizing the device** to prevent interference and keep data integrity intact.

> [!IMPORTANT] Data Integrity
> Maintaining data integrity is crucial because the opposing party's first attempt will always be to refute your analysis, claiming you tampered with or mismanaged the data. You must calculate the **hash of the original artifact** and the **hash of the master copy** you create, proving no data was tampered with and that all analysis was performed on an exact copy.

- **Take photographs** of the drone and its visible components, along with any other visible details, during this phase.

---

## Phase 3 — Identification

Identify the components of the seized GCS:
- Flash storage devices
- Mobile devices
- Remote controllers
- Electronic parts

This step also covers identifying both **physical and digital evidence**.

### File Format Identification

Determine the specific file format of the extracted data:
- `.dat` files
- `.trm` parameter files
- `.tlog` files
- `.ulog` files

> [!NOTE] Open-Source vs Proprietary Controllers
> - **Open-source flight controllers** (e.g., ArduPilot) — analysis is much easier because they do not use heavy encryption.
> - **Proprietary drones** (e.g., expensive commercial models) — contain highly encrypted data. For those, you may need to write separate **R-language scripts** or use **ArduPilot packages** with specialized decoding steps.

---

## Phase 4 — Forensic Analysis

- Create a **forensic image** of the device by copying data **bit-by-bit** to produce an exact duplicate.
- All examination is performed on this copy, never on the original.
- Extractable data includes:
    - Serial numbers
    - MAC addresses
    - Photos / videos
    - Telemetry data

> [!TIP] Prefer Open-Source Tools
> We highly prefer using open-source tools for analysis. **Why?** If the defense counsel also wants a copy to conduct independent forensic analysis, open-source tools make it trivial to prove findings — you can say: *"Run the analysis yourself using these same tools; you will get the exact same data."*
>
> If you use expensive, proprietary tools, the opposing side might argue they don't have access and need extra time (e.g., six months to send it to another lab), causing significant prosecution delays.

### Forensic Reasoning — Inferring Intent from Logs

Once data is extracted, log analysis reveals what actually occurred. **You will not find conclusions written explicitly in the logs — you have to infer them.**

> [!EXAMPLE] Waypoint Navigation — Real-World Case
> An agricultural drone was operating near a defense establishment (a large Air Force station). The operator claimed: *"I was only flying an agricultural drone to spray my crops; the drone drifted over the station on its own."*
>
> **How to prove intent:**
> Examine the logs from **five to ten seconds before the incident** to check whether the drone was still receiving **direct signals from the transmitter**.
>
> - **If signals were being received →** The act was **intentional** — the pilot was in active control; the drone did not drift on its own.
> - **If no signals were received →** The drone may have been operating autonomously or lost connection.
>
> Additionally, check if the **waypoints were deliberately programmed** to pass over the restricted area.

---

## Phase 5 — Reporting

After thorough analysis, the extracted artifacts must be reviewed. A report is generated detailing **every event** from the moment the request for examination was received until the end of the analysis:
- Where the **Chain of Custody (CoC)** was signed
- When and where videos were recorded
- All artifacts found
- Correlated facts and figures for court presentation

> [!IMPORTANT] Reporting Is the Core of Forensics
> Extracting a meaningful conclusion from data is the real work of forensics. This clarity comes only with experience — from the number of cases processed, reports compiled, and iterations when reports are sent back with cross-questions asking why a certain point wasn't inferred or requesting additional details. **The more iterations you complete, the better forensic scientist you become.**

---

## Phase 6 — Presentation in Court

- The forensic lab personnel often present the findings themselves.
- They correlate findings with the specific clause of the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)** that applies.
- Work with **cyber law professionals** to determine which clauses and sections form the legal case.

## Serial Number Identification

> [!WARNING] No Single Serial Number for a Drone
> Unlike a car (which has a chassis number and engine number), **there is no single serial number for the entire drone assembly**. The GCS is not a single product — users often attach different gimbals, cameras, or GPS modules.

- **DJI drones** usually have serial numbers printed on them.
- **Custom-built / unbranded drones** typically have no external serial numbers at all.
- **Best practice:** Use the **flight controller's internal serial number** as the unique identifier for the entire drone.

> *"There is no SN number for the entire product, however, there is SN number for different components. So you could use one component SN number as the unique identifier such as Flight Controller SN number."*

### Tool Notes

- **Magnet AXIOM** — widely used paid tool, relatively easy to acquire in India.
- **Cellebrite** — Israeli product; the most expensive on the market.
- **Binwalk (Entropy Measurement)** — When analyzing a data file, entropy measurement identifies which portions are **compressed or encrypted** vs. simple sequential data. High-entropy (highly random) regions indicate compression or encryption. By pinpointing these sections, you can isolate and decrypt/decompress only the relevant portions instead of processing the entire file. This avoids wasting time on empty or irrelevant data blocks.
- **DatCon** — For DJI drones: first extract raw data, then extract and decode flight logs.
- **CsvView PC** — Visualize decoded logs: flight paths, motor status over time, power-source curves, altitude/height from altimeter logs (climbs, descents).
- **ExifTool** — Extract Exif data from photos/videos captured by the drone camera, yielding **GPS location and timestamp** — proves the drone was present at a specific place and time.
- **DCode** — Decode timestamps from various formats.
- **Google Earth** — Import and superimpose flight paths to visualize the drone's route (e.g., proving it flew over a restricted Air Force station).
- **ArcGIS Pro** — 3D trajectory visualization (proprietary, paid license).

## Collection & Analysis Workflow

### Step 1 — Determine the Problem

Identify what happened:
- **Crash** — drone came in for post-crash analysis
- **Flight into controlled airspace** — near air stations, aerodromes, airports, defense establishments, government organizations
- **Invasion of privacy** — e.g., drone flying over a resort swimming pool to photograph tourists
- **Illegal activity** — transporting drugs, weapons, or contraband

### Step 2 — Gather Extensive Information

- These systems are **highly complex** and can be **highly customized**
- The operator may have installed additional sensors (LiDAR, ultrasonic for collision avoidance)
- Understand exactly what components are installed
- There is **no limit** to what can be integrated — gather information on every component
