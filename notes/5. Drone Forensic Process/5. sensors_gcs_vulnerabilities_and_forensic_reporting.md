## Sensor Types & Their Forensic Significance

The type of sensor onboard reveals the **purpose of the flight**. Beyond flight controller logs, analyze data from all onboard sensors — cameras, LiDAR, collision-avoidance systems — as this data is highly valuable.

| Sensor Type | Forensic Relevance |
|------------|-------------------|
| **Optical camera** | Photos/videos with Exif data (GPS, timestamp) |
| **LiDAR** | 3D terrain mapping — requires GPS for geo-referencing |
| **Near-Infrared (NVIR)** | Night flying capability — requires GPS for context |
| **Thermal camera** | Surveillance, search & rescue — raises intent questions if mounted on a consumer drone |
| **Wi-Fi module** | Communication interception, network data |

> [!NOTE] GPS Integration
> Almost all advanced sensors integrate GPS modules that append location data directly to files. Without GPS coordinates, images lose their geographical context.

### Common Drone Cameras

| Category | Brands |
|----------|--------|
| **Consumer** | GoPro, DJI, Canon, Sony |
| **Pro-sumer / Professional** | Specialized thermal, multispectral, LiDAR payloads |

### Artifact Location

Sensor data (images, videos) is stored on SD cards **directly on the UAV** — pull the SD card to begin extraction.

## EXIF Data Analysis

> *The purpose of a camera is to take a picture, and EXIF data tells a story about the camera and where it was taking pictures.*

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706193522.png)

![](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260706193531.png)

Exif (Exchangeable Image File Format) data extracted from drone photos reveals:

| Field | Forensic Value |
|-------|---------------|
| Image description | Context about the capture |
| File folder path | Storage structure on the device |
| Manufacturer | e.g., DJI — identifies the drone brand |
| Camera model & serial number | Ties a specific photo to a specific camera on a specific drone |
| Creation date & time | Establishes timeline |
| GPS version | Protocol used for location |
| Reference latitude (N/S) | Hemispheric positioning |
| Altitude (above sea level) | Flight height at capture |
| Camera aperture | Shooting conditions |
| Exact GPS coordinates | Proves the drone's location |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Exif data allows you to prove that a specific photo was captured by **this exact camera** mounted on **this specific drone** at a **specific time and place**.

## Real-Time UAV Hijacking (Wi-Fi Vulnerability)

Several commercial UAVs use **Wi-Fi for command & control and data transfer**. This creates an attack surface:

1. **Identify the SSID** of the drone's Wi-Fi network
2. **Deauthenticate the UAV** from its current controller
3. **Capture the UAV's attempt** to re-establish the link
4. Once the new link is established → **control the UAV**, download telemetry, or download sensor data

| Tool | Target | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Skyjack** | AR Parrot drones | Dedicated hijack tool; the same approach works on DJI Phantoms using Wi-Fi |

> You can also hack into other data link mechanisms beyond Wi-Fi.

## Flight Log Visualization & Analysis

### PixHawk-Based UAVs — Mission Planner Visualization

Flight paths from UAVs with PixHawk flight controllers can be visualized in under **two minutes** using Mission Planner. Commercial software superimposes flight paths directly onto 3D maps, showing exact height and trajectory.

> [!TIP]
> Presenting data in visual format (3D maps, altitude plots) makes it much easier for a **judge or jury** to understand how the flight proceeded.

In raw form, the log is just a massive block of binary data (1s and 0s). You must:
1. **Parse** the raw binary
2. **Plot** the graphs
3. **Correlate** the sequence of events over time

### Parameters Found in Flight Logs

| Parameter Category | Examples |
|-------------------|----------|
| Battery | Capacity (mAh), voltage pins |
| IMU | Accelerometer offsets, gyroscope data |
| Servos | Roll and pitch servo positions |
| Flight modes | Altitude Hold, Return-to-Launch, Terrain Follow |
| System | Software versions |

> [!IMPORTANT] Report Annexures
> When compiling the forensic report, **attach logs and graphs as technical annexures** so other experts can verify findings. Analyzing raw data is not a quick task — it requires meticulous, deep-dive analysis, sorting through massive amounts of irrelevant data to isolate key evidence.

## Sample Drone Forensic Report

A drone forensic report is a formal technical document detailing the **recovery**, **extraction**, and **analysis** of digital evidence from:
1. The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV)
2. Its flight controller
3. Its payload (cameras, LiDAR, collision-avoidance, RF sensors)

The report establishes:
- **Flight paths** — exact trajectory reconstruction
- **Operator actions** — what the pilot physically did
- **System anomalies** — battery failure, motor malfunction, in-flight fire, overheating (temperature logs show sharp increases)

### Five Core Sections

| # | Section | Contents |
|---|---------|----------|
| **1** | **Report ID** | Case identifier for indexing (e.g., `25/2025-26` for the 25th case of fiscal year 2025-26) |
| **2** | **Evidence Received** | Date received, who delivered it, who received it, physical condition, serial numbers, photos of physical artifact |
| **3** | **Forensic Tools & Methodology** | Every tool used for imaging, decryption methods, visualization software for 3D flight paths |
| **4** | **Analysis & Findings** | Step-by-step analysis and evidence discovered |
| **5** | **Conclusion** | Final professional opinion: intentional act vs. genuine accident (wind, mechanical failure) |

> [!WARNING] Cross-Examination Defense
> Document tools and methodology carefully. During cross-examination, the defense counsel's primary tactic is to prove the forensic analysis was conducted improperly, arguing the report is technically invalid. Defense lawyers constantly look for **technical or procedural errors** to dismiss the forensic report.

## Challenges & Solutions in Drone Forensics

### Challenge 1 — Evolving Communication Protocols

| Protocol Shift | Implication |
|---------------|-------------|
| Wi-Fi → Bluetooth → Dedicated radio → **LTE / 4G** | Control signals routed through commercial cellular towers; standard RF triangulation becomes ineffective |

- Cellular-controlled drones are **harder to intercept** but **easier to locate** through network records
- Standard forensic tools cannot easily parse cellular-routed data

### Challenge 2 — Vendor & System Diversity

- Many manufacturers, each running **customized embedded systems**
- Countless log formats and highly customized payloads
- Extracting data from diverse systems is increasingly difficult

### Solution — Focus on GCS & Sensor Data

> [!TIP] The 80% Rule
> **Focus your analysis on the Ground Control Station (GCS) and post-processing systems.** Analyzing sensor data and GCS logs provides **80% of the evidence** you need — messages sent to the drone, actions taken, and sensor data recorded. This is where the real story lies.

### Systems Approach to Investigations

> [!IMPORTANT] sUAS — It's a System, Not Just a Vehicle
> The proper legal and technical term is **Small Unmanned Aircraft System (sUAS)** or UAV. "Drone" is not a technical or legal term. The system includes:
> - The physical aircraft
> - The payload sensors
> - The communication links
> - The ground control station
>
> **Do not treat the flying vehicle as an isolated element.** Analyze the entire integrated system.

### Regulatory & Legal Considerations

When applying laws and policies, focus on the **capabilities of onboard sensors and services**, not just the flying delivery mechanism:

- **Why** did they mount a thermal or near-infrared camera? A farmer or blogger has no legitimate need for a high-end thermal camera.
- **Where** were they flying? Did they enter restricted airspace?
- Did they obtain **government clearances** and register their flight plan?
- If the drone weighs **more than 2 kg**, the operator must hold a **valid pilot license**

> [!CAUTION]
> If you only focus on extracting raw data, you will miss the bigger picture. Raw data is one component — used to corroborate logical inferences. The surrounding **regulatory violations**, **licensing issues**, and **flight plans** are equally important in building a solid legal case.
